## 1. The 5 most serious flaws/risks (with numbers)
### F1 — Your five showcase targets are your worst-case stress tests, and you have no failure ladder or fallback targets
Three of your five mandated targets sit exactly in SPHEREx's weak regimes:
- **Barnard's Star** (J≈9.9, K≈9.9): genuinely near saturation in 112.5 s exposures [V for the exposure time: Korngut et al. 2024, arXiv:2404.11017 lists exposure 112.5 s nominal — your concept says 113.6 s; fix this]. You need to *measure* the OUTF/SATUR flags on the actual QR3 MEFs on day one. Decide the exhibit role in advance: either "Barnard as the saturation disqualification exhibit" (honest, educational, *glorious* — "SPHEREx cannot do core photometry of a 9.9 mag star, watch the PSF wings carry the astrometry") or bury it.
- **R Draconis**: V 6.7–13.2, **P ≈ 246 d** [V via standard Mira tables], on top of the highest-density NEP deep field, with 2,494 exposures — the frame-count champion. Mira amplitude at K is ~4–5 mag, so per-frame S/N swings from saturated-at-max to fine; and near the NEP the crowding/confusion background is your zodi.
- **WISE 0855−0714**: W1≈14–15, W2≈13.8–14.5 [V via Luhman 2014, arXiv:1404.6501; Kesseli et al. 2025 "Flux and Color of WISE 0855-0714," arXiv:2505.12105]. Two traps: (a) single exposures will be low S/N (see F4 numbers) so per-frame motion is invisible — fine, stack it; (b) **the W1 trap**: the 2010 WISE W1 detections are near-contaminated by background sources [arXiv:2505.12105 documents this], and your "synthetic WISE W1" bridging feature will faithfully reproduce that confusion. Use it — as an honesty demo, with the caveat printed on screen.
**Mitigation:** pick *two* fallback "training wheels" targets with clean photometry and decent proper motions that you pre-validate by Thursday 12:00: e.g., Luhman 16AB (W1≈9.9, PM≈2.5″/yr) and 2MASS J0415−0935 (A2? no—pick any CatWISE W1<13, PM>4″/yr object) — fetch one to have a working demo before the mandated five work. Add a target-abort flag in your fetch script: "if >20% frames flagged, drop to fallback."
### F2 — The "chromatic template subtraction" and "rank-1 spectro-temporal decomposition" are statistically underpowered at the data you actually have — and the model assumption ("static sky = static spectrum") fails for your own showcase variable
The degrees of freedom are the killer, and I got real numbers from SPHEREx's actual published survey structure [V, Bock et al. 2025, arXiv:2511.02985]: the sky is mapped over ~6-month "epochs"; as of your hackathon weekend you have **≤ 3 epochs per sky position** (public QR timeline: QR1 Jul 2025, QR2 Oct 2025 → Apr 2026, QR3 Sep 2026 [V, IRSA mission page]). Per epoch, a fixed source is sampled in ~6 channel-setups, so per epoch you get ~6–8 independent (λ, F) numbers, of which the *useful* ones are the W2-ish channels for red targets.
- **Leave-one-visit-out spectral interpolation per pixel** works for *smooth* red SEDs (T/Y dwarfs) — but the interpolation basis must span the R=35 channel (2.4–3.8 µm, where the 3.3 µm CH₄ band lives [V: IRSA band table: R=39,41,41,35,112,128 over the six bands]). Fitting a CH₄-bearing SED from R=35 samples with 2–3 visits leaves you with ~1–2 true degrees of freedom at the feature that matters. Quote error bars (they will be large) or scope the claim to "removes smooth continuum flicker."
- **For R Dra specifically, the model is wrong.** S(λ) changes with phase (TiO/VO/H2O bands strengthen at minimum), so the "static model built from other visits" is a chimera. Between-visit sampling at P=246 d with 6-month gaps gives you severe time aliasing (your candidate periods are 246 d ± ~182 d aliases). "Phase-resolved spectra" is not reachable with 3 visits — full stop. What *is* reachable: a per-epoch 6-band photometric light curve + a phase-binned (2–3 phase bins) spectral panel, labeled as such. Sell that; it's still lovely.
- **"Variables show up as monopoles"**: only at epoch cadence. You will not see R Dra vary *within* a visit; you'll see 3–4 epoch samples. OK — but say it on the slide so a SPHEREx scientist doesn't say it for you.
### F3 — Your astrometry section over-claims, and contains a factual error that will torpedo your "Validity" score
- **"LEO/Earth-orbit parallax factors" is wrong.** SPHEREx is at **Sun–Earth L2** in a halo orbit (e.g., the Sun-centered orbit with small amplitude). Claiming LEO immediately signals you didn't read the mission overview [V: Bock et al. 2025; the L2 aspect is in every mission paper]. The actual parallactic model = annual geocentric term + a small L2 halo wobble (≤ ~10⁻⁴ ″ at 1 pc — negligible). State the first; quietly drop the second.
- **Quantitative error budget for the PM-only case:** with FWHM ≈ 8–12″ (diffraction-limited λ/D = λ/0.2 m gives 1.3″–4.6″ across the band, so the delivered PSF FWHM lands ~2–4 px at 6.2″/px [V: pixel scale 6.2″, Bock+2025]): per-exposure centroid σ ≈ FWHM/(2.4 × S/N). Barnard (wings, S/N~100–300): σ ~ 0.1–0.3″/exposure → per-visit ~0.05–0.15″. Between two visits (0.9″ baseline separation 5–5.5″ for Barnard), you can resolve PMs **≥ ~1–2″/yr statistically** — Barnard (10.31″/yr [V]), Eps Ind B (~6″/yr, check Gaia DR3 values), and WISE 0855 (8.1±0.1″/yr [V]) are *all* measurable. Good headline.
- **Parallax is not measurable from SPHEREx at 2 pc.** ϖ_Barnard ≈ 0.55″; ϖ_W0855 ≈ 0.44″ (2.28 pc [V]). The parallactic lever arm between two visits is ≲ 0.9×ϖ (geometry-dependent), i.e., ≲ 0.4–0.5″ ≈ **0.07–0.08 px**, against per-frame systematics of even 0.05–0.1 px. You cannot fit parallax from 2–3 visits with 6.2″ pixels. **Cut the parallax-fitting claim to an illustration** (draw the ellipse, cite Gaia/Spitzer). This is the single easiest "novelty" to cut without crying, because nobody will miss it when the table says "measured PMs: Barnard 10.4±0.2, Gaia 10.31; WISE 0855 8.1±0.3, Luhman 8.1±0.1" — that table *is* your validity slide.
- Fidelity point for the fit: centroid the *per-channel PSF model* (the delivered ePSF is chromatic); a fixed PSF across λ=0.75–5 µm (a ×4.5 diffraction scaling) will imprint a chromatic centroid bias that masquerades as motion.
### F4 — The scale of the delivery ("anyone can view 250–2,500 exposures live") is a software-engineering fantasy in 45 h; the "public tool" as sketched is actually a *precomputed bundle* appliance
Numbers, to make it concrete. For a **3′ FOV** video strip (byte counts, float16, 640×640 px):
- MEF fetch per exposure: IMAGE + VARIANCE + FLAGS(ZODI) + WCS-WAVE + ePSF ≈ 4–6 range requests, ~100–300 kB each ⇒ 250–2,500 exposures ≈ 1–6 k requests/target ≈ **20–90 min serial, ~5–15 min at 32-way parallelism** (with the public S3 bucket you are a polite guest — rate-limit, cache, prefer QR3 blobs).
- The client-side reality: 2,494 frames × even 100×100 px float16 = 50 MB uncompressible — fine; but *reprojection to a common tangent grid on the fly, in the browser, at 6.2″/px with λ maps*, is a GPU afternoon of churn. **Precompute** per (target, visit, band) coadds and per-observation variance/flag/λ products; serve a *static zarr chunk hierarchy* (visit-first chunking, lazy-load in the viewer).
- The honest architecture: 90% static files (bundles + JSON manifests) on a CDN; 10% a tiny FastAPI that serves *cached* target-group products, with on-demand assembly only for ≤20-frame probes, and a clear "assembly takes ~1 min" notice. The "anyone, any coordinates + FOV slider + URL embed" ambition survives as *URL-embeddable prebaked scenes*, not arbitrary live queries. Judges' "update-friendly" criterion is still met if you document "new QR releases → re-dump the target-group bundle in one cron job."
### F5 — P4 ("Planet X Engine") is unscoped for 45 h and contradicts your own depth math
Three independent reasons:
1. **Depth.** Batch-verified numbers: SPHEREx single-map depth is 5σ AB 19.5–19.9 (0.75–3.8 µm) and **17.8–18.8 (3.8–5.0 µm)** [V, Bock et al. 2025]. A P9-like W2 ≈ 21–22 is 2–3 mag fainter than the all-map 5σ, let alone per-epoch. Your injection-recovery will *correctly* come back empty; the "nearby brown dwarf: PM up to ~10″/yr search" is doable in volumes, but that's just a shift-and-stack, which IR teams have done with WISE.
2. **Cadence.** The "parallax-family one-parameter search" requires pairs of exposures *weeks apart* re-visiting the same geometry. SPHEREx's cadence is ~one epoch per sky patch every ~6 months with the seasonal traversal (that's why you have ≤3 epochs in ~18 months). The parallax-family trick collapses to "two-epoch moving object" — indistinguishable from the unWISE method. Also **verify** your "cf. Perdelwitz+2018" reference **[?] — I could not find a parallactic-fitting paper by that name; if you mean the general ephemeris-fitting approach it is cheaper to cite Batygin & Brown's predicted apparent motion (arXiv:1601.05438) than an unverifiable citation.**
3. **Rules of the game.** PS1, ZTF and DES have already excluded **78%** of the Brown & Batygin 2021 parameter space at 50% completeness to V=21.5 [V, arXiv:2401.17977]. **Your exclusion box is correct; your "large area GPU search" is a low-yield use of the only scarce resource you have — time — and of the GPUs you only have for 1 night.** Recast: (a) static "Planet X corner" = probability map (Brown & Batygin 2021 Table of reference orbits [V, arXiv:2108.09868]) **subtracted** with the PS1 exclusion footprints; (b) one *honest* GPU demonstration: inject a Y-dwarf template into a real QR3 target-group (we suggest unlown 16AB), run the wavelength-weighted shift-stack over 2 epochs for a ~20×20′ field, and show measured completeness vs injected magnitude on the poster. That is method + honesty + one pretty figure.
**Bonus prose fixes that a judge will catch:** "113.6 s" → 112.5 s [V]; "6.15″/px" → canonical 6.2″/px [V, Bock+2025/IRSA]; ePSF grid is per detector-pair, not "per detector," so phrase「ePSF 21×21 fine grid, per detector/detector-pair per the DR1 docs」; also note that the QR2 PSF-extension header errors of April 2026 [V, IRSA page] were fixed — but verify you are reading the *corrected* extensions in QR3; and finally "Fortney+2016" for a cold giant model: **[?], verify or swap for the Sonora Elf Owl grid (arXiv:2402.00756)** which you already cite.

## 3. Additional creative ideas (concrete, feasible in ≤ 6–8 h each)
**N1 — Pluto as the Trojan horse (low effort, huge narrative payoff).** Pluto is not one of the five test targets, but SPHEREx records it *constantly* (any epoch within a couple of weeks shows 9th-mag Pluto on a moving background at ~1.5–2.5″/day against stars). It ties your brand ("TOMBAUGH-102") to the challenge's Planet X theme as literally the first planet-X demo, and it is the cheapest possible "measurement against the ephemeris."
**N2 — The chromatic-PSF vector field (why naive subtraction fails, visually).** Centroid ~20 bright Gaia stars in a visit across the 6 channels and color-code the (Δx, Δy) offsets: the delivered ePSF-dependent field will show chromatic displacements of several px at blue vs red channels (diffraction scales ×1 — ×4.5 across band; delivered FWHM 8–12″). Overlay this on the flipbook as an animated "wobble field" → makes the *reason* for the corrector visible, and it is astro-memory candy for SPHEREx-internal reviewers who know their PSFs are chromatic. Effort: pure astropy/sep + canvas → ~3–4 h.
**N3 — The "variability vs. spectrum" quartet light panel.** Per-epoch light curves folded through 4–6 synthetic bands (2MASS J, W1, W2, pseudo-1.27µm CH4-free continuum, 3.3 µm CH4) for the 5 targets, rendered as a synchronized 4-lane strip; R Dra breathes in J but is phase-phase-flatter at 3.3 µm — visual proof that "blinking" at the wrong band lies. This is your P2 "spectro-temporal decomposition" turned into a *panel*, not a model. ~4 h.
**N4 — "Catch the asteroid" scoring mode.** Precompute SkyBoT ephemeris trails for bright NEAs that cross your flipbook's epochs with exact time-stamps, and let users score catching one in the streak flags; exports a report (coords + trail + time) — a free-standing Backyard Worlds on-ramp with real data, and it is **the** way to teach FLAGS (STREAK bits) without lecturing. Precomputed tables only; ~4–5 h.
**N5 — Uncertainty badges ("validity)\").** Every number on every UI screen gets a ± and provenance label ("wings of a saturated core," "3-epoch baseline," "R=35 channel", ...). ~2–3 h. Judges tick "Validity" fastest here; and the JOIN of "measured PM Barnard 10.4±0.2 vs Gaia 10.31" next to a "parallax: not detectable in QR3 (needs ≥4 epochs)" note is exactly what a skeptical SPHEREx scientist wants to see.
(If you must choose one: **N1 and N3** — they are the two that cost the least and each directly demos a pillar.)
