# Codex (gpt-6-luna) as judge: the one-frame version, 15 Nov 2026

Prompt: three critiques (public and product; space science; software) of the shared frame, the Sky map (planets, "My sky now", SPHEREx by date, place card) and the 3D time regimes. Unedited output below; what was checked and adopted is in `docs/07_si_usage_log.md`.

1. **Public member and product designer — [web/src/skymap.ts](<web/src/skymap.ts:52>), `openSky()` and `renderSub()`; [web/src/shell.css](<web/src/shell.css:42>), rail and dock styles.** Within ten seconds, the map looks polished but still asks a newcomer to decode terms: “movers,” “102 colours,” “not in Gaia,” “WISE years,” and an unlabeled date slider. “Click a point … or any place” is only a transient hint, hidden on phones. The rail offers seven competing starting points, and the dock’s 1999→2010→2025→today→+1yr scale mixes survey dates with a future planet forecast. The cards then introduce magnitudes, ecliptic, Galactic coordinates, and exposure counts. “SPHEREx, live” is misleading: the date-indexed coverage is built from a static exposure index, while “live” mostly means that the map updates its date or fetches current cube tiles; the newest image may lag the index. The single biggest impact change: make the default experience a guided, plain-language prompt—“Pick a place to see how it changed”—with one obvious example and an immediately visible image comparison. Put the research layers behind a secondary control.

2. **Space scientist — [web/src/ephem.ts](<web/src/ephem.ts:1>), `bodiesAt()`, `whenSeen()`, `zodiac()`; [web/src/skymap.ts](<web/src/skymap.ts:112>), `reveal()` and `myView()`.** The orbital positions are appropriately described as approximate for a whole-sky map, but the magnitude language overreaches: the listed “classic V” coefficients and phase correction are not uniformly calibrated across planets, and naked-eye thresholds depend on sky conditions. The apparent locations are geocentric, while the “My sky now” visibility calculation does not account for parallax (especially relevant to the Moon), refraction, observer elevation, or twilight; “rises about” is also a five-minute search using a date-only decimal year, not a local clock-time ephemeris. `lstAt()` approximates sidereal time from a decimal year and omits UT1 and precession consistency; acceptable for rough orientation, but not precise rise/set advice. `reveal()` interprets sampled coverage cells as a smooth, cumulative sky fill; the generated coverage compresses exposures into visits with a 20-day gap and may omit exposures due to the upstream per-cell sampling. It does not establish an official survey “pass” number. The “pass N of the sky” label is especially misleading: it uses a mean visit count, rounded up, not a numbered all-sky pass. Finally, `zodiac()` assigns constellations from ecliptic longitude thresholds even though IAU boundaries are not constant ecliptic-longitude slices; its “IAU boundaries” claim is false. Label these as rough estimates and remove the pass wording or use actual IAU boundary data.

3. **Software reviewer — [web/src/skymap.ts](<web/src/skymap.ts:120>), coverage loader; [web/src/placeview.ts](<web/src/placeview.ts:48>), `loadSpherex()`; [web/src/space.ts](<web/src/space.ts:511>), `setRegime()`.** The coverage fetch catches failure silently and leaves `cov` null; playback then presents a generic “SPHEREx” state without telling users the data file is missing. In `loadSpherex()`, visits with fewer than two frames are discarded, and if all are discarded the card says no exposure is listed—even when exposures were listed but unusable. `toGrid()` falls back to a nearest pixel when interpolation samples contain NaNs, but does not bounds-check that rounded coordinate, so edge pixels can read outside the array. In `space.ts`, `planetsLive()` schedules timeouts guarded by `capTok`, but `setRegime()` itself does not cancel an in-progress `tweenTime()` animation; a pending tween can overwrite the newly selected regime’s time after a user switches modes. On phones, the rail can occupy 56vh while the dock wraps to two rows and the card can cover 74vh; together these controls leave little map visible, and a card hides both rail and dock, making its timeline unavailable until dismissal. Add explicit loading/error states, distinguish “listed but unreadable” from “none listed,” bounds-check the fallback sample, and cancel or supersede time tweens on regime changes.

**Five changes I would make first, in order:**

1. Replace the default rail-first experience with one clear action and a visible example of the before/after image comparison.
2. Replace “SPHEREx, live” and “pass N” with precise language about indexed exposure dates, sampling, and freshness.
3. Correct the zodiac mapping with real IAU constellation boundaries.
4. Make the “My sky” result explicitly approximate, account for the Moon’s topocentric position, and avoid presenting rough rise times as reliable observing advice.
5. Fix and surface the coverage and image-loading failure states, guard `toGrid()` at its edges, cancel stale time animations, and rework the phone layout so the timeline remains accessible with a card open.
