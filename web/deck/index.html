01 · The challenge

The sky moves. Almost nobody can watch it.

NASA's SPHEREx photographs every place in the sky every six months, in 102 colours of infrared light. Anything close to us that moves is in those pictures: cold brown dwarfs, and a ninth planet if there is one.

NASA asked for a public tool that plays a place through time like a little video, so that what moves is easy to spot. Today that takes a specialist, an archive query and a pipeline.

02 · What we built

One time bar for the whole sky.

  • Search anything, even misspelled: a planet, a star, a kind of object.
  • Drag the time bar from 1999 to today: three NASA surveys, then SPHEREx filling the sky on its real dates.
  • Planets live, and your own horizon: what is up tonight.
  • What the sky is made of: point anywhere and read its spectrum.
  • Click any place to watch it through time, or to measure it.

The frame on the right is the product itself, running. Use it.
