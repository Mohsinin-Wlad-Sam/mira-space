# High-level summary (150 words, 846 characters)

MIRA is a public web observatory on NASA's SPHEREx. Point anywhere in the sky and it plays 27 years of NASA pictures of that place as a film (1999, 2010, every SPHEREx visit), so whatever moved is plain to see. Then it measures it, in your browser, on NASA's raw exposures: how fast it moves, what its light says across 102 infrared colours, whether it changed. Every number carries its error and is checked against ESA's Gaia: Epsilon Indi B reads 4.70 arcseconds a year (Gaia: 4.68), with methane in its spectrum.

Behind it is a whole-sky survey: 17,604 moving stars confirmed on raw SPHEREx data, 635 of them in neither Gaia nor SIMBAD, which anyone can help examine. A blind test found 92% of known movers and raised no false alarm. AI models search and estimate, and are labelled as such. No install, no account; open API and documentation.
